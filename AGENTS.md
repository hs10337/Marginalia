# Mimir

Mimir is a personal assistant that helps people remember the context around the
people in their lives. This file is the source of truth for the brand and the
design principles it implies. Read it before making UI, copy, or visual
decisions.

---

## Brand Idea

**People are made of details.**

Mimir helps users remember the context around the people in their lives.

The brand should feel: personal, warm, perceptive, quiet, contemporary,
gender-neutral, human, not technological.

Mimir is not about storing memories. It is about **paying attention to people
over time**.

---

## Brand Promise

**Mimir helps you remember the context that makes relationships meaningful.**

The emotional benefit:

> You can be more present because you do not have to hold everything in your head.

Mimir should support relationships without making them feel optimized,
quantified, or managed.

---

## Positioning

Mimir is **not** a:

- Personal CRM
- Contact database
- Second brain
- Memory vault
- Productivity dashboard
- Digital scrapbook

Mimir is:

> **A quiet assistant that helps you remember what matters about the people you know.**

---

## Brand Personality

- **Perceptive** — notices small but meaningful details.
- **Warm** — human, but never cute, sentimental, or overly soft.
- **Quiet** — intelligence appears through usefulness, not constant AI messaging.
- **Curious** — observant, interested, slightly unexpected.
- **Contemporary** — editorial and culturally current rather than futuristic or nostalgic.

Key tensions:

| Avoid       | Mimir        | Avoid      |
| ----------- | ------------ | ---------- |
| Nostalgic   | Contemporary | Futuristic |
| Sentimental | Warm         | Clinical   |
| Cute        | Human        | Corporate  |
| Scrapbook   | Montage      | Dashboard  |
| Mystical    | Perceptive   | Brainy     |
| Loud        | Quiet        | Invisible  |

## Visual Concept

### The whole emerges from fragments.

Knowing someone happens cumulatively through conversations, preferences,
moments, changes, and follow-ups.

The visual system should reflect:

**Fragment → connection → recognition**

Use: cropping, overlap, reframing, sequencing, reflection, obstruction, visual
proximity.

Avoid making fragmented faces the single recurring motif. The **behavior** of
assembling context is the identity.

---

## Photography

Photography should feel **observed rather than staged**.

Look for: partial portraits, tight crops, hands and gestures, people entering
or leaving frame, reflections and glass, motion blur, negative space,
architectural framing, unexpected compositions, image sequences.

The feeling should be:

> **There is more to this person than what you are seeing right now.**

Avoid: generic lifestyle stock photography, posed smiling friend groups, soft
beige wellness photography, polaroids, postcards, scrapbooks, and nostalgic
artifacts, literal brain, neural-network, database, or memory imagery.

---

## Graphic Language

Use **frames, crops, montage, and sequencing**.

Layouts can feel slightly misaligned or interrupted rather than perfectly
systematic.

Think: editorial montage, film editing, sampling, cropping, visual juxtaposition.

Avoid: craft collage, torn paper, tape, scrapbook aesthetics, rigid dashboard
grids.

---

## Color

- **Background:** `#F7F6F2` — warm off-white.
- **Text:** `#181818` — near-black.
- **Primary accent — Mimir Blue:** `#3046C5` — deep ultramarine. Confident, contemporary, gender-neutral.
- **Secondary accent — Orange:** `#FF5A1F` — energetic and human. Use sparingly.

Principle:

**Blue = identity. Orange = interruption.**

Warmth should come primarily from photography and people, not a large pastel
palette.

---

## Typography

Use a contemporary sans serif or grotesk with subtle character.

Typography should feel: editorial, clear, confident, human.

Avoid: futuristic/geometric tech fonts, rounded "friendly" fonts, handwriting,
typewriter aesthetics, overly nostalgic serif systems.

---

## Motion

Motion should reinforce:

**Fragment → alignment → recognition**

Use: crops moving into alignment, reframing, reveals, sequential imagery,
simple cuts and transitions.

Think **film editing**, not computer processing.

Avoid: glitches, neural animations, data streams, particle effects, AI shimmer.

---

## Product UI

### Prioritize people over data

People should not feel like records in a CRM.

Surface natural context:

> "Sarah mentioned she is starting a new job next month."

rather than:

> "Life Update · Employment · August 12"

Keep AI machinery quiet. Avoid unnecessary extraction labels, confidence
scores, technical states, and metadata.

Suggestions should feel natural:

> "You might want to ask Alex how the move went."

not:

> "AI Recommendation Generated."

---

## Voice

Mimir sounds like someone who pays attention and knows when to speak.

Voice should be: observant, understated, direct, human, helpful.

Good territory:

- **You remembered.**
- **Pick up where you left off.**
- **Keep the thread.**
- **Remember the context.**
- **People are more than profiles.**
- **The little things matter.**

Avoid:

- "AI-powered memory companion"
- "Second brain for relationships"
- "Never forget anyone again"
- "Relationship CRM"
- "Optimize your relationships"
- "Remember everything"

---

## Creative North Star

**Mimir is not about memory. Memory is the mechanism. Attention is the value.**

> **Mimir should not look like where memories are stored. It should feel like what it means to know someone over time.**

When evaluating a design, ask:

- Does it feel contemporary rather than nostalgic?
- Human rather than technological?
- Warm without becoming sentimental?
- Personal without becoming invasive?
- Does it communicate attention rather than storage?
- Is the AI quiet?
- Does it treat people as complex rather than as data?
